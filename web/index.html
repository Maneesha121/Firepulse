Method and limits

VIIRS (375 m) detects many more small fires than MODIS (1 km), so raw counts are not comparable. VIIRS months are divided by a calendar-month ratio estimated where both sensors operated (2012 onward), giving a MODIS-equivalent index. The shaded band is the inter-year IQR of that ratio. The ratio is estimated for the whole region and applied to your area, so small areas are less reliable. Anomalies compare a month with the same calendar month in other years (median and MAD). Detections include agricultural burning and other heat sources. Months before 2012 cannot be checked against VIIRS.

Sensor Intelligence: how observed detections change across the MODIS-to-VIIRS transition

Read this first. Everything here compares observed satellite detections. A difference between MODIS and VIIRS counts is not a difference in how much actually burned. Ratios are observed detection ratios for this area and period, not universal sensor correction factors. The harmonised index is a MODIS-equivalent index, not a physical fire count. Months with no usable data are shown as gaps or “unavailable”, never as zero.

How to read these charts

A. Raw MODIS and VIIRS counts differ in scale because the sensors differ in resolution and detection behaviour. The harmonised index divides VIIRS months by the calendar-month ratio so that it is on the MODIS-equivalent scale. It is an index, not a count of fires. Raw combined is simply MODIS + VIIRS and mixes two different measuring scales.

B. For each calendar month, the median of VIIRS/MODIS over usable overlap months in the selected dates; the band is the 25th to 75th percentile. Usable = both sensors observed and MODIS count ≥ 5. Fewer than 5 usable years: no ratio is drawn. 5–9 years: exploratory. The dashed line shows the whole-record median for comparison.

C. The same ratio month by month. Hollow points are months with 1–4 MODIS detections: the ratio exists but is unstable and is not used. Months with zero MODIS detections have no ratio. The stepped line is each year’s median ratio (needs 6+ usable months). A drift may reflect platform mix, season, or area size rather than sensor behaviour.

D. Share of observed detections per platform. It is not a share of fires. Platforms observe over different periods; unavailable months are missing, not zero.

E. Each overlap year is held out, the ratio is fitted on the other years only, and MODIS counts for the held-out year are predicted from its VIIRS counts. sMAPE is 0–200%; absolute bias is |predicted − actual| in total detections. Correlation within a year mostly reflects the seasonal cycle, so a high value does not prove a good fit. Years with too few valid months show no metrics. Compare with the climatology column (median MODIS of the same month in other years).

F. How much the observed ratio moves between time windows and between platform pairs. It is a spread of estimates, not a confidence interval. All thresholds are engineering choices (see docs/methodology.md).
